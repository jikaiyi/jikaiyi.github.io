Bio

I am an Assistant Professor in Computer Science and Engineering at the University at Buffalo. Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, and a Visiting Student Research Collaborator in Electrical Engineering at Princeton University. I received my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from The Ohio State University, supervised by Prof. Yingbin Liang, and my B.E. from the University of Science and Technology of China.

I received the NSF CAREER Award (2025), CSE Excellence in Research Award (2025), SEAS Early Career Researcher of the Year Award (2026), and UB’s Exceptional Scholar Award – Young Investigator (2026).

Research Interests

My research addresses a central question in fundamental AI: how can intelligent systems continually acquire, organize, and transfer knowledge while adapting across tasks, objectives, and environments? I pursue this question through principled learning frameworks for foundation models, agentic systems, and embodied intelligence, grounded in the mathematical foundations of bilevel and multi-objective optimization. My work spans three interconnected directions:

Adaptive Foundation Models

  • Principled methods for multi-task and continual adaptation of foundation models through selective parameter sharing, optimized subspaces, and knowledge-preserving updates, with the goal of improving knowledge transfer, reducing catastrophic forgetting, and balancing competing objectives during post-training.
    ACL ’26 GRID ICML ’26 ECCV ’26 DeepMTL2R Library

Agentic and Embodied Intelligence

  • Self-organizing, decentralized multi-agent agentic systems in which agents coordinate, adapt, and share knowledge without a central orchestrator, and Imperative Learning, a self-supervised neural-symbolic framework that integrates perception, reasoning, planning, and control for robot autonomy.
    IJRR ’25 Book Chapter Imperative Learning Series NSF SODA

Bilevel and Multi-Objective Optimization

  • Scalable, provably efficient algorithms and complexity lower bounds for bilevel and multi-objective optimization, which provide the foundation for multi-task, continual, and meta-learning methods that balance conflicting objectives, allocate resources fairly, and transfer knowledge across heterogeneous tasks.
    ICML ’21 JMLR ’22 NeurIPS ’23 ICML ’24 arXiv ’26
