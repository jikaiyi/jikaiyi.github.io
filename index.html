Bio

I am an Assistant Professor in Computer Science and Engineering at the University at Buffalo. Previously, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor, and a Visiting Student Research Collaborator in Electrical Engineering at Princeton University. I received my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering from The Ohio State University, supervised by Prof. Yingbin Liang, and my B.E. from the University of Science and Technology of China.

I received the NSF CAREER Award (2025), CSE Excellence in Research Award (2025), SEAS Early Career Researcher of the Year Award (2026), and UB’s Exceptional Scholar Award – Young Investigator (2026).

Ph.D. Opening: I am looking for one Ph.D. student to work on (multi-agent) agentic systems. Applicants with strong mathematical foundations and coding skills are particularly encouraged to apply. Please email me your CV, transcript, and relevant publications (if any).

Research Interests

Our goal is to develop principled learning and optimization algorithms that advance artificial intelligence, embodied and robotic systems, and machine learning, bridging the gap between theoretical foundations and real-world applications. Our work is organized around three connected directions:

Large Foundation Models

  • LLM agents, long-context multi-step RAG, generative recommendation, continual learning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and token selection for LLM post-training.
    ACL ’26 ICML ’26 ECCV ’26 NSF SODA

Embodied Intelligence

  • Neuro-symbolic robot learning, long-horizon robotic tasks, multilevel optimization over hybrid discrete–continuous spaces, and derivative-free methods for control.
    Automatica ’21 IEEE TAC ’24 IJRR ’25 Book Chapter iSeries

Theoretical Foundations

  • First- and zeroth-order algorithms, adaptive gradient methods, with recent emphasis on bilevel and multi-objective optimization.
    ICML ’21 NeurIPS ’23 ICML ’24 ICLR ’25 Nested Chain ’26

Recent News

  • September 2026DICE was accepted to NeurIPS 2026. It decouples whether an LLM tutor should intervene from what it says, and learns a selective intervention policy via KL-regularized policy optimization weighted by a rollout-grounded Intervention Value, cutting over-intervention to near zero while guiding students to correct solutions in 3–4 fewer turns.
  • August 2026New collaborative work with Lesi Chen and Jingzhao Zhang introduces a novel nested-chain construction yielding the best-known lower bounds for bilevel optimization, near-optimal for quadratic lower-level problems. It also recovers the recent tight block-chain lower bound by GPT Pro for highly smooth nonconvex optimization with a much simpler derivation (paper).
